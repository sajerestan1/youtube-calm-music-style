# Omazmusic album workflow

**Read this file before every new album. This is the current source of truth.** Older notes in the README and album logs are historical; follow this workflow if an older note conflicts with it.

## Album start: five topic choices

When the user says **“create the next album”** without naming a topic:

1. Check the current repo and recent Omazmusic album notes so the next idea is distinct from earlier albums.
2. Research current YouTube examples in the calm/acoustic music niche. Use the user's reference channel, [Acoustic Haven](https://www.youtube.com/@acoustichaven_2026), and other relevant successful channels. Look at recent titles, thumbnails, listener use cases, descriptions, and visible performance. Treat views as a snapshot, not proof of cause.
3. Offer **exactly five** distinct album topic concepts. Make them about real listener situations or moods, such as a train ride home, a quiet evening after work, a slow Sunday morning, reading at home, or an unhurried drive. Explain each in one short line.
4. Stop and let the user choose. Do not generate tracks or artwork before they choose. If the user already names a topic, skip this choice and continue.

Do not copy a channel's exact titles, lyrics, melodies, artwork, character, or branding. Learn from the way successful channels frame a listener's need and make original Omazmusic material.

## 1. Make the album music

After the topic is chosen:

1. Create a new album record in `albums/` and use a separate project for this album. Never alter an earlier album's Canva design.
2. Choose a working album name and prepare a tracklist of original songs that fits the selected topic. Give the album, tracks, project, and exported video clear, descriptive names based on the listener use case and real music. Do not use only Omaz/Omazmusic or an album number as the filename. Keep track names distinct from prior albums.
3. Create tracks in Clone Voice **one at a time**. In the lyrics field, use only lyric text and ordinary punctuation: no brackets, braces, asterisks, Markdown, or section labels. Put sound/style directions in the style field.
4. Save and confirm each finished audio file before starting the next generation. Record its exact filename and measured duration in the album record.
5. Keep creating tracks until their measured combined duration is **at least 1:30:00 and less than 2:00:00**. Aim for about **1:58:00**. Canva exports must stay below two hours. Ten tracks is not a fixed limit. If the tracks are too short, make more. Do not estimate from the track count or stop early.
6. Check file playback and durations. Build a track-order sheet with each start and end time. The final start/end list must agree with the actual audio sequence.
7. Upload the album's tracks to Canva. Upload only tracks made for this album. Add the files in a controlled way and save the Canva design.

If a track generation or upload truly requires the user's action, explain the exact step and resume from the completed files. Do not claim that a file is created, uploaded, or verified until it is visible in the relevant app.

## 2. Ask which visual treatment to use

**After the album tracks are uploaded to Canva, ask the user to choose one option before making or placing the main visual:**

1. **Animated artwork:** create a new scene with the recurring Omazmusic character and animate it in Canva. Make three short, gentle animation clips from different viewing angles while preserving the same recognizable face and channel identity.
2. **User-supplied video:** the user uploads the chosen video and places it on the Canva timeline. Then extend or duplicate it to cover the full music duration.
3. **Still artwork:** create a new image for this album and place it on the Canva timeline.

Wait for the user's choice. Do not choose for them or start a different visual treatment while waiting.

For every option, keep the recurring young man recognizable, including his face and headphones. Change his pose, clothing, activity, and setting for each album. Use the channel banner palette and a bright, warm, welcoming scene. Do not reuse an old album image as the new album's main artwork. For video clips, mute the clip audio so it cannot compete with the music.

## 3. Assemble the Canva timeline

Use a **new Canva design and timeline for each album**. Keep the existing album open in its current tab; do not open duplicate tabs for the same project.

1. Set the project to YouTube landscape, **1920 × 1080, 16:9**.
2. Put all album tracks in playlist order on **one straight audio lane**. Place them end-to-end. Do not stack tracks vertically or leave them overlapping so multiple songs play together. A brief intentional crossfade is acceptable only when the edges are controlled and the transition sounds clean.
3. Check each join before continuing. Confirm the full music sequence is at least **90 minutes** and record its exact endpoint. If it is short, add more original tracks before finishing the visual timeline.
4. Put the chosen image or video on the video lane and extend/duplicate it to the exact audio endpoint. If using animated artwork, use the three distinct clips in sequence and cover the full duration.
5. Treat the waveform as optional and wait for the user's direction. Once the audio and main visual cover the full timeline, ask whether the user wants the waveform added. If there is no reply within five minutes, skip the waveform and continue to export. If the user says they will add it, leave it out and resume after they say it is ready. When approved for you to add, place the transparent, animated waveform as a separate visual overlay near the bottom. Extend it from the beginning to the music endpoint. Keep it small enough not to obscure the scene, and mute its audio.
6. Add Canva's **Match and Move** transition between alternating visual clips, then use Apply to all when available. Check the joins and final endpoint.
7. If sung-lyric subtitles are requested, keep them on their own subtitle lane and sync each caption to the words heard in that song. Preview the captions against the audio at the start, middle, and end of songs. Move only captions that are actually mistimed; do not shift the entire caption sequence based on one sample. For instrumental passages, do not create speech captions or invented lyrics. Accurate track-title cards may be used at verified song starts instead.
8. Save after meaningful edits. Wait for Canva to show that changes are saved. Preview the beginning, several joins, the middle, and the end. Confirm audio, visual, waveform, and any subtitles finish at the intended time.

## 4. Export and check the actual file

1. Export from Canva as a **720p MP4** and save it to the user's Desktop when the download prompt allows a location choice. Use a descriptive, search-friendly filename that states the listener use and accurate runtime, such as `calm-beach-walk-music-gentle-songs-1-hour-58-minutes.mp4`. Do not use a brand-only or album-number-only filename.
2. Wait for the download to finish. Open the exported file and check its actual duration and playback near the start, middle, and end.
3. The delivered video must be **at least 1:30:00 and shorter than 2:00:00**, with a target near **1:58:00**. The Canva timeline ruler alone is not proof. If the export is too short or reaches two hours, correct the Canva timeline, save, and export again.
4. Record the exported filename, measured duration, and any issue in the album record.

## 5. Research and package the YouTube upload

After the export is verified:

1. Search YouTube and the web for current examples of successful videos in this niche. Use the user's reference channel, [Acoustic Haven](https://www.youtube.com/@acoustichaven_2026), when relevant. Compare several real titles and thumbnails, including the listener situation, title wording, thumbnail text, duration, and description/chapter style.
2. Write an original title that a person might actually search for and that accurately describes this album and its use. Avoid vague internal track names and do not repeat “relaxing” in every album title. Never claim instruments, vocals, mood, or duration that are not in the audio.
3. Write a natural description with the real album details and **verified timestamps/chapters** from the final audio. Start chapters at `00:00`, use exact track titles and measured start times, and ensure the final chapter list matches the exported file. Do not guess times. Add relevant credits and a few accurate search phrases.
4. Make a separate, bright, attractive thumbnail using the **same recognizable Omazmusic character shown in this album's video**. Keep the person and image quality consistent with the video. Use clear, high-contrast text with careful placement and include the real duration only if it fits the design.
5. Add specific, accurate tags for this video's actual music, mood, use case, and setting. Follow the tag checklist in §7.
6. Upload to the **Omazmusic YouTube channel**. Schedule it for **48 hours after the latest already scheduled Omazmusic video**, using the correct `Europe/London` local time (including GMT/BST for the date). Check the newest scheduled video first; do not calculate from the current upload-ready time. Check the title, description, thumbnail, tags, audience, visibility, date, and displayed UK time before confirming.
7. Verify in YouTube Studio that the video is scheduled and record its link, schedule, and status in the album record.
8. Schedule a Community announcement for each scheduled video to publish **30 minutes after** its confirmed video release time. Convert and set the post date/time in the same local timezone used for the video, and state the video’s release time in UK local time in the announcement. Include the watch link and accurate release details. For videos already public and more than 30 minutes past release, publish the announcement now. YouTube schedules posts by date and time; this is a manual offset, not an automatic video-publish trigger.

The user has asked for this standard Omazmusic upload and 48-hour UK scheduling workflow. Follow it for each album and report any step that genuinely needs the user's input or action.

## 6. Record the work so it resumes cleanly

For each album, update its file in `albums/` with:

- chosen topic and research references
- track titles, filenames, durations, order, and measured chapter times
- total source audio duration and verified export duration
- chosen visual treatment and asset filenames
- Canva design link and saved timeline end
- thumbnail, YouTube title, description/chapter list, and scheduled date/time
- video-specific tags and Community post text, watch URL, post URL/status
- current state and the single next action if work pauses

At the end of a session, save in Clone Voice, Canva, and YouTube Studio where applicable. Do not leave a track half-generated or a timeline change unverified and describe it as complete.


## 7. Video-specific tags and Community announcement

For every video upload:

1. Add tags that describe the actual album: its listener use case, music style, vocal/instrumental content, mood, and setting when those are audible or visible. Use the final audio and video as evidence. Do not reuse an unrelated tag set, add misleading terms, or pad tags with repeated keywords. Keep tags within YouTube's field limit and verify the saved tag chips before leaving Details.
2. For every scheduled video, create a short Community post for that specific video and schedule it for **30 minutes after the confirmed release time**. Set the post date and time in the same timezone as the video, converting correctly across date boundaries and daylight saving changes. Mention the video’s release time in UK local time, the listener use case, and album; invite viewers to listen and include the watch link. Keep the wording natural and accurate; do not make unsupported claims or promise results. For videos already public and more than 30 minutes past release, publish the announcement now.
3. Use the watch URL from the current upload. Verify the post is in YouTube Studio's Scheduled section and confirm its date/time is exactly 30 minutes after the video. The post is scheduled independently from the video; there is no automatic publish trigger.
4. Verify the post appears in the Omazmusic Community tab and record its link or status in the album record.

Community posts are required for every scheduled or published video. When asked to announce previously scheduled videos, inspect YouTube Studio and create or schedule a post for each video that has not already been announced. Do not duplicate an existing announcement.
